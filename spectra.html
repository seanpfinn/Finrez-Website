Spectra

Industry Industrial Analytics
Deliverables Branding Web Design

A lattice identity for an agentic analytics platform — and the product marketing site built on it. Spectra models an industrial operation as a knowledge graph and ships agents that traverse it, from a raw sensor reading to a dispatched work order. Finrez was brought in for the identity: a mark, a deck, and an asset set ready for immediate use.

The Challenge

Every platform in the category promises accuracy, which is a claim nobody can check. An engineer evaluating monitoring software has read the same sentence on five other sites that week, and has no way to test any of them. Spectra needed a line someone in that position would actually believe — and a visual system restrained enough that a single deviation could break it on purpose.

"No silent failures."

— the brand line

It doesn't claim the system is always right. It claims that when it's wrong, you will know. A promise about a failure mode can be tested; a promise about quality can't, which is why the second kind never survives contact with an engineer.

Direction & Thinking

The product is the thesis: it plots every reading and flags the one that doesn't belong. So the mark isn't drawn, it's plotted — an H routed between five points on a five-by-eleven lattice, with a node at every intersection and no optical adjustments anywhere. Anyone can rebuild it from the coordinate table, which is the test a plotted mark passes and a drawn one doesn't.

That constraint is what makes the rest of the system work. Every point in the identity sits on an intersection precisely so that one thing can break it — which means the brand needs no separate warning language. An anomaly is simply a point that has left the lattice.

System & Touchpoints

Five colour values, and only four of them are surfaces. The fifth, anomaly oxide, isn't a colour in the usual sense — it's a state, and one rule governs it: orange means a point is off its lattice, right now. Nothing else may borrow it. Put it on a button and you teach an operator to ignore the one colour that matters. Graphite holds roughly 85% of any surface, paper and soft ink the next 12%, cyan and oxide the remaining 3%.

Two typefaces, three weights, assigned by size rather than emphasis. Geologica carries language; Azeret Mono carries values, and only values — timestamps, readings, labels, never a sentence — so a readout can never be mistaken for prose. Alongside them the system ships the parts a brand book usually only describes: six mark alternates, the motion timings, a five-state mascot built from a single lattice node, an app icon, and thirteen PowerPoint layouts where every chart is a real chart object.

Outcome

Three deliverables came out of one decision about what Spectra is, which is why the mark, the deck and the site hold together. The product marketing site opens on the product rather than a promise — a live agent-activity feed, a pressure chart with one breach hatched above its threshold, and a fleet view of four hundred assets where exactly one point wears orange.

The Site

The marketing site had to carry the same argument the console does, which meant every page needed a device drawn out of the identity rather than applied to it. The console page runs a rail down its left margin that is the traversal itself, advancing one node at a time as you read. Pricing draws its tiers as lattice density — the thing being priced is points on a graph — and every tier still has exactly one oxide dot, because the number of things worth looking at does not scale with the number of things watched.
